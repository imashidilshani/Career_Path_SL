Business Analyst

Overview

A Business Analyst (BA) is an IT professional who connects business needs with technology solutions. Business Analysts understand organizational problems, gather requirements from stakeholders, analyze business processes, and help development teams create software solutions that provide business value.

Business Analysts play an important role in software projects because they ensure that technical teams understand what users and organizations actually need. They work between business users, customers, project managers, developers, designers, and testers.

In modern IT organizations, Business Analysts help improve business processes, identify opportunities, define software requirements, and support successful project delivery.

For IT undergraduates, Business Analysis is a valuable career path because it combines technical knowledge, communication skills, business understanding, software engineering concepts, and analytical thinking.

Key Responsibilities

A Business Analyst is responsible for:

- Understanding business problems.
- Gathering requirements from stakeholders.
- Analyzing existing business processes.
- Preparing requirement documents.
- Creating user stories.
- Defining software features.
- Communicating with development teams.
- Supporting software testing activities.
- Validating implemented solutions.
- Identifying process improvements.
- Managing requirement changes.
- Creating business reports.
- Supporting project decision-making.

Role of a Business Analyst in Software Development

Business Analysts work throughout the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC).

Requirement Phase:

Responsibilities:

- Understand customer needs.
- Identify business goals.
- Gather requirements.
- Analyze problems.

Design Phase:

Responsibilities:

- Explain requirements to developers.
- Review system designs.
- Provide business feedback.

Development Phase:

Responsibilities:

- Clarify requirements.
- Support developers.
- Manage requirement changes.

Testing Phase:

Responsibilities:

- Review test cases.
- Perform requirement validation.
- Support User Acceptance Testing (UAT).

Deployment Phase:

Responsibilities:

- Confirm business requirements are satisfied.
- Support users during system implementation.


Requirement Engineering

Requirement engineering is the process of identifying, documenting, and managing software requirements.

Types of Requirements:

Functional Requirements

Describe what a system should do.

Examples:

- User registration
- Login functionality
- Payment processing
- Report generation


Non-Functional Requirements

Describe system quality attributes.

Examples:

- Performance
- Security
- Reliability
- Scalability
- Usability


Requirement Gathering Techniques

Business Analysts collect requirements using different methods.

Interviews:

Direct discussions with stakeholders.

Workshops:

Group sessions to identify requirements.

Surveys:

Collect information from many users.

Observation:

Understanding existing business processes.

Document Analysis:

Reviewing existing documents and systems.


Requirement Documentation

Business Analysts create different documents.

Business Requirement Document (BRD)

Describes business objectives and high-level requirements.

Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

Defines detailed system requirements.

User Stories

Describe features from the user's perspective.

Example:

"As a customer, I want to track my order so that I know the delivery status."

Acceptance Criteria

Defines conditions that determine whether a feature is complete.


Business Process Modeling

Business Analysts analyze and improve business processes.

Important concepts:

Business Process Mapping

Visual representation of business activities.

Process Improvement

Finding ways to make processes faster and more efficient.

Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN)

A standard method for representing business workflows.

Summary

Business Analysis is an important IT career path focused on understanding business needs and converting them into technology solutions. Business Analysts require strong communication skills, analytical thinking, documentation abilities, and technical knowledge.

IT undergraduates interested in Business Analysis should learn requirement engineering, Agile methodologies, SQL, software development concepts, and business process modeling. Building practical case studies and gaining internship experience will help students become successful Business Analysts.
